Locate conduit before concrete work

Conduit locating in Castle Rock is for projects where a planned opening will pass through a concrete slab or wall. Conduit may be embedded in the concrete, so a surface check alone won't show its route. A scan can give your crew a clearer picture of the work area before they choose where to drill, core, or saw cut.

This service focuses on features within concrete. It isn't a search for buried utility lines across a yard or property. If your project involves opening concrete, arrange a scan before the work starts, while there is still time to review the marked area and adjust the plan if needed.

Why hire Recon GPR

Recon GPR provides independent, third-party concrete scanning and subsurface locating. We mark clear safe-zones directly on site, so your crew can see the scan information where the work is planned. That can be especially useful when the person doing the concrete work wants a separate scan rather than relying only on assumptions about what is inside the slab or wall.

A scan is information for planning, not a replacement for the judgment of the crew carrying out the work. Tell us what opening you have in mind and what kind of concrete surface is involved. For conduit locating in Castle Rock, it helps to explain whether the job is a core, a cut, or a drilled opening.

What the scan covers and how to prepare

The scan can map embedded conduits, rebar grids, and post-tension cables. Recon GPR marks the mapped features on site, giving the crew a visual reference as they plan the work. The specific area to scan should match the planned opening and the space around it that your crew needs to consider.

Before the appointment, gather a few details:

  • The site address and a contact for the person meeting the scanner.
  • The location and approximate size of the planned opening.
  • Whether the work involves drilling, coring, or saw cutting.
  • Any available drawings or notes about the concrete, if you have them.

Avoid choosing a drill or cut location based only on where it looks clear from above. Don't treat a concrete scan as general utility locating for the surrounding property, either. Call a scanning professional when the work will penetrate concrete and the location of embedded features is uncertain.
