Before work starts in Castle Rock

GPR scanning in Castle Rock gives you useful information about items that may be inside concrete before a crew makes an opening. That can help homeowners and project leads have a more informed conversation about where to core, cut, or drill. The scan is performed on the concrete surface, and detected findings can be marked on site for the crew to review.

Scanning is worth discussing when you have a specific opening planned but don’t know what may be beneath the surface. It helps you avoid treating an unseen slab or wall as an empty work area. It does not replace the judgment of the people doing the concrete work, so share the results with your contractor before work proceeds.

Why call Recon GPR

Recon GPR provides independent third-party concrete scanning and subsurface locating. That means you can bring in a scanning provider separately from the crew carrying out the core, cut, or drilling work. The goal is to give that crew clear information from the scan area, rather than asking them to guess where embedded items may be.

Before booking, be ready to explain what you want to do and where. A few helpful details include:

  • The concrete surface and approximate work location
  • Whether the planned opening is a core, cut, or drill
  • Any plans or information you already have about the area

If your plans change, tell the people involved so the scan findings can be considered in the context of the new location.

What the scan covers

Recon GPR maps rebar grids, post-tension cables, and embedded conduits. Findings and clear safe-zones are marked directly on site so the crew can see the scan information where the work is planned. Ask what area will be scanned and how the markings will relate to your proposed opening.

A simple preparation checklist can keep the visit focused:

  • Make the intended work area accessible.
  • Identify the approximate spot and opening size for your contractor.
  • Share any available drawings or details about the concrete.
  • Have the person planning the work available to confirm the location.

Don’t move the opening after scanning without checking with your contractor and discussing whether the new spot needs to be reviewed. A mark on one part of the concrete doesn’t describe a different part of the surface.

Local planning in Castle Rock

For a Castle Rock project, start by confirming the exact surface and opening location with the crew doing the work. Recon GPR serves Castle Rock as part of its coverage across Denver, the Front Range, and Colorado mountain communities. If your project is in town, give the team the address and explain what the crew needs to do, so the scan can be discussed in relation to that specific work area.

Don’t wait until a crew is ready to drill to bring up scanning. Call when you’re planning the opening and can describe the location. That gives you time to coordinate access and make sure the people involved understand the markings.

Talk through your project

When you contact Recon GPR, have the work location and planned opening details handy. Ask about scanning the specific concrete area and on-site marking of detected findings and clear safe-zones. If you’re unsure what information to provide, describe the job in everyday terms and the team can discuss the scan needs with you.
